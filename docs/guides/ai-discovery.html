AI discovery and public crawler access

How we publish clear, crawlable information about Davonium Technologies for search engines and compatible AI retrieval systems.

Our public information

We publish public company information, service descriptions, current product status, and technical documentation on our canonical domain. The linked HTML pages are our primary source; our llms.txt and llms-full.txt are supporting indexes.

We keep product status consistent across the homepage, Products page, product guides, README, and machine-readable summaries. We do not publish private customer records, credentials, internal project data, or secret configuration to increase discoverability.

Crawler permissions

Our robots.txt permits OAI-SearchBot for ChatGPT search discovery and separately permits GPTBot. OpenAI documents these crawler roles independently: OAI-SearchBot supports search discovery, while GPTBot relates to OpenAI’s model-training crawl. We review these choices against our content and data-use preferences.

A robots rule is not access control. Cloudflare firewall rules, bot controls, HTTP errors, redirect behaviour, or an incorrect canonical route can still prevent a public crawler from fetching a page. When access fails, we check the actual production response and hosting rules.

Machine-readable company summary

We maintain llms.txt as a concise company and product index and llms-full.txt as an expanded map of our public pages and guides. We keep these files aligned with canonical URLs and the product status shown in HTML.

We treat these files as optional conventions, not a universal indexing standard. Search engines and AI providers may ignore them, so we also maintain crawlable HTML links, descriptive titles, canonical metadata, accurate visible content, and an XML sitemap.

Indexing, citations, and limitations

For Google, our release checklist includes verifying our domain property in Search Console, submitting the XML sitemap, and inspecting important URLs. We can also configure our domain in Bing Webmaster Tools. These steps make discovery easier to manage, but they do not force a search engine to index a page.

We follow the current OpenAI crawler documentation, OpenAI publisher guidance, and Google’s crawlable-link guidance. We cannot guarantee crawling, indexing, ranking, AI summaries, citations, or worldwide visibility.

What we maintain

We keep this page, robots.txt, llms.txt, llms-full.txt, and our product-status pages aligned. When a crawler or platform changes its published guidance, we review our implementation rather than assuming discovery is automatic.
