Search discovery and indexing

How we make our public pages understandable and crawlable for search engines and compatible AI retrieval systems.

How our pages are discovered

We publish each company page and documentation guide as an ordinary HTML document. Essential content and links are present in the HTML response; our documentation search only filters the published links. People and crawlers can follow those URLs without relying on a JavaScript-only navigation action.

We use https://www.davoniumtech.com/ as our canonical public domain. We keep internal links, canonical tags, Open Graph URLs, structured data, and sitemap entries aligned with that domain and our clean routes.

  • We use descriptive link text so people understand where each link leads.
  • We link relevant guides from product and service pages so that important pages are discoverable without an external search.
  • We fix broken internal links when we find them.

Metadata and structured content

We give important pages topic-specific titles and summaries. We add canonical and social-preview metadata and use Organization, WebSite, or TechArticle structured data only when it accurately describes visible content.

Structured data helps machines interpret information already visible on a page; it does not guarantee a rich result, indexing, ranking, or citation. We follow Google’s guidance on crawlable links and canonical URLs when reviewing our website.

  • We keep titles and descriptions specific to each page.
  • We make structured data agree with the visible page and current product status.

Sitemap and crawler access

We publish robots.txt and an XML sitemap containing our canonical public routes. We allow OAI-SearchBot to fetch public pages for ChatGPT search discovery and exclude error and offline utility pages from our preferred sitemap and routine indexing.

Our robots rules express crawl preferences; they are not access controls. Cloudflare firewall settings, bot controls, HTTP errors, redirects, or a wrong canonical route can still prevent a crawler from fetching a public page.

We also publish llms.txt and llms-full.txt as optional indexes for compatible tools. These files do not replace crawlable HTML, useful content, canonical metadata, or the sitemap, and we do not assume every provider reads them.

Measurement and realistic expectations

After verifying our domain property, we use Google Search Console to submit the sitemap and inspect priority URLs. We can also register our domain in Bing Webmaster Tools and use its reporting after the property is configured.

After important releases, we check production status codes, canonical URLs, sitemap entries, page titles, internal links, and crawler access. When a route changes, we update its links and sitemap entry together.

Search engines and AI services independently decide whether to crawl, index, rank, summarise, or cite our pages. We cannot promise that any URL will appear for a query or be included in an AI answer. We focus on accurate, original information and make our public pages easy to access and verify.
