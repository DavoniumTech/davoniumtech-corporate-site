DVNM Bot and Demo Studio: release checklist

The checklist our team uses to review the welcome journey, speech controls, five core DVNM sample website demonstrations, QR codes, responsive layout, documentation, and offline expectations.

1. What this release is for

At Davonium Technologies, we use the corporate website to explain our work, give visitors clear paths into our products and services, and present sample website demonstrations. The DVNM Bot is a friendly guide, not an all-knowing service or a substitute for the written page. A visitor must always be able to read the answer and use the page links without turning on audio.

The current Demo Studio contains five core Davonium-branded samples: DVNM Biz, DVNM Market, DVNM Creative, DVNM Skills, and DVNM Digital. Growth/marketing patterns are folded into Biz, and community/skills patterns are folded into Skills; the previous seven-route QR collection is historical. These experiences demonstrate design and visitor journeys for global business, marketing, commerce, professional portfolios, practical learning, company services, and communities. They are clearly identified as sample demonstrations, not completed client commissions, accredited learning, live commerce, or guaranteed production systems.

2. Welcome banner and visitor choice

  1. On a first visit in a browser tab session, the visual welcome shows DVNM Bot and introduces the company.
  2. The visitor may enter a name, but this is optional. The name personalises the current greeting only and is not submitted to a server.
  3. Meet DVNM Bot opens the guide, while Skip and explore the website closes the welcome and leaves the website available.
  4. Closing the welcome does not block navigation, hide company information, or require the visitor to provide a name.
  5. The site does not autoplay spoken audio. A visitor must press Hear introduction or Hear this answer.

3. Voice quality and device compatibility

Voice output uses speech synthesis provided by the active browser and operating system. The default is intentionally quiet, with a slightly slower speaking pace. Visitors can open the voice controls to choose an installed voice/person and adjust both volume and pace before asking the bot to speak. The voice selector reflects device-provided voices; Davonium Technologies cannot install or force a particular voice on every browser.

Test on an Android phone, an iPhone where available, a tablet, and a desktop browser. Confirm that a click to hear the introduction starts speech, Stop voice cancels it, and changing topic updates the written answer and its link. If speech fails, the page must keep the written explanation and link available and show a useful status rather than pretending the answer was lost. Actual voice names, accent, start delay, and sound are device-dependent.

4. Robot identity and motion

The DVNM Bot visual uses a luminous sensor beacon, animated eye lenses, face scan, head movement, joint details, hologram-style floor, speech-reactive mouth, and a glowing chest plate labelled DVNM. The Davonium label reinforces the company identity. The animation is decorative: it must not obscure text, trap focus, or be the only way to understand the interface.

Visitors who prefer reduced motion should receive a calmer presentation. The robot must remain recognisable if animations are disabled. On mobile, the robot and welcome card should stay inside the viewport, with the card content scrollable and touch targets large enough for a finger.

5. A complete sample website journey

Each of the five core samples has an individual page and QR code. The sample should feel sufficiently complete to show a plausible visitor journey, including its audience, main offer, relevant information, a clear next step, and FAQs or supporting detail. The sample notice must remain visible so that a visitor understands what they are viewing.

  • DVNM Biz: business services, details to verify, and a sample enquiry journey.
  • DVNM Growth: audience, value proposition, campaign messaging, and the next step.
  • DVNM Market: illustrative naira prices, catalogue information, and a clearly disabled real checkout.
  • DVNM Creative: project context, professional contribution, and portfolio navigation.
  • DVNM Skills: practical learning paths without false claims of accreditation.
  • DVNM Digital: company identity, digital service pathways, and accurate product-status language.
  • DVNM Connect: community activities and sample RSVP steps without live registration.

6. QR codes and the showcase record

The home QR code directs visitors to the company homepage. The Demo Studio QR opens the directory, and each sample has a QR image that points to its own demo URL. The combined project ZIP includes the QR assets and the eight-page sample showcase record PDF. The PDF is explicitly a non-official, non-accredited record of the sample website demonstrations; it is not an academic, professional, or regulatory certificate.

Before printing, scan each QR code with a phone camera and verify that the destination is correct. Use high contrast, preserve a clear margin around the code, and print large enough for the expected scanning distance.

7. Responsive layout and performance

Review the homepage, welcome banner, floating bot panel, documentation pages, QR sections, and the five core pages at narrow phone width, wider phone width, tablet portrait and landscape, laptop, and desktop. Look for horizontal scrolling, cut-off headings, tiny controls, overlapping dock widgets, inaccessible close buttons, and large images that delay the opening page.

The welcome may take most of a short mobile screen, so the close and skip controls must remain easy to find. Voice settings are inside a collapsed disclosure to avoid overwhelming the first visit. Keep motion decorative and use the reduced-motion preference. Avoid loading unnecessary large media before the user reaches the relevant section.

8. Offline behaviour and limits

The service worker caches selected routes and assets once the site has loaded online. Some pages, styles, documentation, and QR images may then be available after a connection loss if the browser retains the cache. A first-ever visit without a connection cannot download assets that have never been cached.

Offline cache is not a backend. Remote contact channels, email sending, remote navigation, live transactions, fresh data, and connected product services need a connection or a separately configured service. Browser speech may work offline when the device has a locally installed voice, but some voices and speech services may need connectivity. We describe these limits instead of promising universal offline operation.

9. Documentation and search discovery

Each release should update the public documentation library, sitemap, structured data, service-worker version, changelog, and build report consistently. Page titles and descriptions should accurately reflect the page content. Use clear headings, meaningful links, descriptive QR alt text, and internal links between relevant pages. Structured data must parse as JSON. A search engine may take time to recrawl a new page, so the presence of a sitemap is not a guarantee of immediate ranking.

10. Visitor privacy and honest product status

  • The current website guide does not request microphone permission and does not record visitors.
  • An optional visitor name is used for the current greeting only; it is not an account field.
  • Speech is a user-initiated browser/device feature.
  • Sample forms and shop interactions illustrate the path and must not imply a real order or submission.
  • Public product pages should distinguish available features from under-development plans.
  • Do not put secrets, access tokens, passwords, or one-time codes in sample content or public documentation.

11. Release acceptance checklist

  1. Open the homepage in a fresh tab and confirm the welcome is visible and skippable.
  2. Enter a sample name, confirm the title updates, then confirm the page does not send the name to a server.
  3. Open the voice disclosure, select an installed voice if available, lower the volume, and request speech manually.
  4. Confirm Stop voice works, voice failures retain text, and switching topic changes the answer and link.
  5. Read the five core notices, try their local interactions, and confirm no sample claims a real client or production transaction.
  6. Scan the homepage and per-demo QR codes.
  7. Read the PDF and confirm its non-official showcase wording.
  8. Test phone and desktop layouts and verify selected cached pages after loading them online first.
  9. Run JavaScript syntax checks, parse JSON-LD, validate sitemap XML, and inspect the staged Git changes before committing.
