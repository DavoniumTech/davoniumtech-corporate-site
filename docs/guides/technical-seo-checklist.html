Technical SEO checklist

The checks we use for crawlable routes, page metadata, structured information, and search-console readiness.

Crawlability and page status

We keep important content reachable through ordinary links, ensure that public addresses open directly, and provide a clear missing-page response for invalid routes. We check the live domain as well as our editorial records because a page that looks correct in a draft is not proof that the published address works.

We verify the homepage, products, services, contact, documentation index, and representative individual guides. We also confirm that crawler guidance does not unintentionally block the public information we want people to find.

Preferred addresses and sitemap

Every public page has a descriptive title, a useful summary, and a canonical address on our preferred domain. The XML sitemap lists the public pages intended for discovery rather than alternate or duplicate versions.

Canonical tags and sitemap entries are signals, not commands. Search services make their own crawling, canonical, and indexing decisions. We keep internal links, page metadata, redirects, and sitemap addresses consistent.

Content and structured descriptions

We use headings that accurately describe the page, meaningful alternative text for informative images, and structured descriptions that match what a visitor can actually read. We do not invent ratings, reviews, awards, availability, or product claims to seek enhanced search results.

AI discovery and monitoring

Our crawler guidance permits relevant search services to request public company and product information. We separately express our preference about training crawls. These rules are crawl preferences, not access control, and they do not guarantee an AI citation.

After a release, we review Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools for sitemap processing, crawl access, canonical issues, and any specific reason a page is excluded. We act on the reported cause instead of repeatedly submitting an unchanged URL.
