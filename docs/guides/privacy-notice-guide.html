How we maintain a privacy notice

Our approach to keeping privacy information aligned with the actual website, product features, and data flows.

Start with the real service

We base privacy information on the systems and behaviours we operate, not on a generic policy template. The notice needs to reflect what is collected, why, where it goes, who can access it, and how long it is kept.

A privacy notice cannot make an unimplemented process true, and copying another service’s language can mislead visitors.

  • We map every form, account flow, analytics script, and storage mechanism.
  • We identify third-party providers involved in the visitor journey.

Explain collection and purpose

We describe each meaningful category of information and connect it to a purpose. We keep optional and required fields distinct and avoid vague statements that suggest unrestricted reuse.

If the purpose or data changes, we review the notice at the same time as the product change.

  • We explain browser storage separately from server-side account data.
  • We avoid requesting sensitive information through ordinary support channels.

Use clear language

We write for the person using the product. We explain retention, sharing, contact routes, and available choices in direct language and avoid promises that the technical system cannot deliver.

A form that opens an email client is described as an email handoff, not as a stored support ticket.

  • We label draft policies clearly while they await review.
  • We keep contact details and product names consistent across our website.

Review before publication

Our privacy and terms pages remain draft text until we check them against real production practices and applicable legal requirements. This public guide is not legal advice.

We re-check the notice when new providers, account features, payments, messaging, analytics, or data-retention processes are introduced.

  • We retain a record of material policy edits.
  • We publish changes only after the described behaviour and ownership are confirmed.

Before we publish a final notice

We compare policy wording with our actual forms, hosting, browser storage, third-party integrations, retention, and support processes before adopting it.
