Meet DVNM Bot: welcome, voice and privacy

How our animated guide introduces itself, personalises the current visit, reads answers on request, and helps people reach the right part of Davonium Technologies.

1. The first-visit welcome

When the company website is first opened in a browser tab session, DVNM Bot presents a visual welcome banner. It introduces itself as the Davonium Technologies visitor guide and explains how to explore products, services, the five core sample website demonstrations, documentation, QR codes, and contact information.

The banner is designed to be welcoming without taking control away from the visitor. The visitor may press Meet DVNM Bot, choose Skip and explore the website, or close the banner. It is shown once per tab session to reduce interruptions while moving between the site's pages.

Speech never starts automatically. The button labelled Hear introduction is an explicit request, which also helps browsers that require a user gesture before audio can play.

2. Optional visitor name

The name field is optional. When supplied, the name is used to personalise the greeting for that page session only. The feature does not create an account, send the name to a server, use it as an authentication credential, or save it in a persistent profile. Visitors who prefer not to share a name can leave the field blank or skip the welcome entirely.

3. Detailed, curated answers

DVNM Bot provides longer written explanations for six guided topics: the company, product status, web and digital services, sample demonstrations, documentation and offline access, and contacting the team. Each answer is paired with a relevant navigation link. This is a curated guide, not an open-ended generative AI system, so it should never invent product availability, customer results, prices, or company promises.

The five core DVNM pages are described as sample website demonstrations. They show possible design directions and interaction ideas; they are not represented as completed client commissions or as fully operational commercial systems. Shop, contact, and event interactions remain examples unless a page explicitly identifies a production service.

4. Voice choice, volume and pace

Spoken output uses speech synthesis supplied by the current browser and operating system. The guide starts at a quiet 15% volume setting and a calm 0.86× pace, with a control range so the visitor can adjust the level before listening. The visitor may open Choose your voice and volume before listening directly in the welcome, or use Voice and speaking settings in the bot panel, to choose among voices installed on that device, adjust speech volume, or change pace.

Voice names and availability are not controlled by the website. The welcome now includes voice, volume, and pace settings before the introduction is played. Voice availability varies by device; one phone may provide a local English voice, while another offers UK English, US English, or a device-default voice. The recommended option looks for an appropriate English voice and prefers a calmer named voice when one is available. We do not claim identical sound on every phone, tablet, laptop or browser.

Device media volume, Bluetooth routing, silent settings, background audio behaviour and installed language packs remain under operating-system control. If speech fails, the written response and page link remain usable, and the interface explains how to retry. A Stop voice control cancels output on request.

5. Robot animation and accessibility

The robot uses animated CSS details: a pulsing antenna, luminous face lenses, a glowing DVNM chest plate, subtle shell lighting, a hologram-style floor, blinking, waving and mouth movement during spoken output. These effects support the company identity and are decorative rather than essential to the answer.

On small screens, the welcome card changes to a compact layout with a scrollable details area and large touch targets. The visitor can close the welcome, and the bot panel can be closed with Escape. Reduced-motion settings disable non-essential animations where implemented. Text answers remain available without voice, which supports silent environments and people who prefer to read.

6. Offline access: what can work

Davonium's progressive web app service worker stores selected pages and assets in the browser cache. After the visitor has loaded the site online, the homepage, selected documentation, sample pages, styling and some QR images may remain available during a later connection loss. The website cannot guarantee offline availability until caching has completed and the browser keeps the cache.

Offline cache is not a backend. Email delivery, WhatsApp, remote links, hosted product services, server-backed forms, checkout, fresh content, and every dynamic action require connectivity or the corresponding service. Speech synthesis may work offline if a suitable device voice is installed locally, but some voices require downloading or network access. We therefore describe only selected cached pages as potentially available offline.

7. Privacy, honest wording and support

  • The current guide does not use a microphone and does not record visitors.
  • Optional names stay in page memory and are not submitted to a server.
  • Speech uses device/browser speech synthesis and does not require a paid external voice API.
  • The visitor can choose not to use voice and can dismiss the welcome.
  • Sample demonstrations must be clearly called samples, not claimed as client deployments or live commerce systems.
  • Documentation and public product status should be kept consistent with what the site actually offers.

For questions, use the contact page or email support@davoniumtech.com. Never send passwords, one-time codes, API tokens, or private keys.

8. Release verification checklist

  1. Open the homepage in a new browser tab and confirm the visual welcome appears once.
  2. Check both Meet DVNM Bot and Skip and explore the website.
  3. Try a name and verify the greeting updates without any request to an external service.
  4. Use Hear introduction on a phone with its media volume set low, then try Stop voice.
  5. Open voice settings and confirm that available device voices populate the dropdown.
  6. Test all six guided topics and ensure their page links resolve.
  7. Open the five core pages and confirm the sample notice and QR codes are visible.
  8. After loading pages online, test selected pages offline and record exactly what remains available.
