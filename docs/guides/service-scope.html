Service scope and project boundaries

How we define what a website or digital delivery includes, what remains outside the work, and how changes are agreed.

Define the outcome

We begin with the user need and the deliverable: pages, interactions, content, integrations, ownership, and acceptance conditions. A shared scope prevents misunderstandings about what a finished delivery includes.

We separate requirements that are agreed from ideas that remain optional or subject to a separate decision.

  • We record the core pages, target devices, and required behaviours.
  • We identify the person responsible for providing content, credentials, and approvals.

What we include and exclude

We make the boundary of the work clear. A static website can include public pages, links, client-side interactions, and PWA installation; it does not automatically include server-side accounts, a ticket database, payment processing, or ongoing monitoring.

We avoid implying that a button or visual form creates a backend capability unless that service is actually connected and tested.

  • We document third-party services and dependencies.
  • We state whether hosting, domain registration, content updates, or maintenance are included.

Change control

When a request changes the agreed outcome, we review its impact on time, cost, security, accessibility, and future maintenance before implementing it.

A useful change record explains the request and decision instead of silently expanding scope.

  • We keep significant changes traceable in source control.
  • We retest existing features that a change could affect.

Acceptance and handoff

We compare the final result against the agreed acceptance conditions and record remaining limitations. The handoff identifies ownership, deployment instructions, and the support path.

Ongoing support depends on the scope that was actually agreed; we do not imply a service level that has not been established.

  • We check key links and page routes before sign-off.
  • We confirm who can maintain and publish future changes.

Confirming scope

We record the agreed deliverables, exclusions, responsibilities, and acceptance criteria before expanding a project beyond its initial brief.
