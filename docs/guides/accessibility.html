Accessibility checklist

How we design and check the site so people can navigate it with different devices, input methods, and access needs.

Why access matters

We want visitors to understand our products and use our website without being blocked by a particular device, pointer, or sensory preference. Accessibility is part of how we evaluate a useful experience, not a visual layer added at the end.

Our design choices

We keep headings meaningful, labels clear, contrast readable, and line lengths comfortable. Primary actions use familiar controls and have visible focus. Our appearance follows the visitor’s device preference unless they choose a different theme.

Motion is decorative. It never carries information that someone cannot access without animation. We respect reduced-motion settings and keep essential content available when a reveal effect is not used.

How we review accessibility

When we change navigation, forms, theme controls, product cards, or installation assistance, we check the common path and the recovery path. We use automated checks as a starting point, then review keyboard operation, zoom, narrow screens, and focus visibility.

We keep important content available without hover and do not make a visitor depend on animation to find a next step. When a third-party service controls part of an interaction, we explain that boundary and offer a clear alternative where practical.

What we keep improving

We treat accessibility as ongoing work. When a visitor reports a barrier, we ask for the page, device, browser, and action that caused difficulty. We avoid asking for passwords, one-time codes, or private account information in a public support report.

For help, use our contact page or read the support guide.
